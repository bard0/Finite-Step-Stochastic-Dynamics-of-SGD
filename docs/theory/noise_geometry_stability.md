# Noise Geometry and Finite-Step Stability

## Scalar exact model

Consider a local stochastic recursion whose conditional second moment has the form

\[
\mathbb E[x_{t+1}^2\mid x_t=x]
=
\left[(1-\eta\lambda)^2+\eta^2G_\Sigma\right]x^2
+\eta^2\sigma_0^2.
\]

The second moment is stable iff

\[
q=(1-\eta\lambda)^2+\eta^2G_\Sigma<1.
\]

Solving `q=1` gives

\[
\boxed{\eta_c=\frac{2\lambda}{\lambda^2+G_\Sigma}}.
\]

The additive-noise control has `G_Sigma=0` and gives the classical scalar boundary `2/lambda`.

## Finite-sum realization

The noise-geometry term can be generated by sample-dependent quadratic perturbations whose mean cancels in the population objective. Thus the separation is realizable by a finite-sum SGD problem rather than only by an externally imposed SDE noise field.

## Multidimensional direction

For a quadratic loss and quadratic Lyapunov function `V(theta)=theta^T P theta`, the exact conditional drift is

\[
\mathbb E[V_{t+1}\mid\theta]
=
\theta^T A_\eta^T P A_\eta\theta
+\eta^2\operatorname{Tr}(P\Sigma(\theta)),
\qquad A_\eta=I-\eta H.
\]

If the covariance field has a quadratic spatial component, the associated Hessian of `Tr(P Sigma(theta))` contributes directly to the quadratic growth operator. This yields an LMI-style sufficient stability condition without assuming that Hessian and noise geometry commute.

This multidimensional program is mathematically natural but still requires a sharp finite-sum experimental validation before being promoted to a main empirical claim.
