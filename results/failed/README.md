# Failed and negative results

Negative and inconclusive results are preserved because they constrain the scientific narrative and prevent unsupported claims.

A key retained example is the residual-beyond-second-order test: after the second-order Hessian–covariance mechanism was validated, the additional residual mechanism was not independently resolved across seeds. That result motivated the shift toward zero-fit finite-horizon covariance prediction rather than forcing a higher-order spectral narrative.

Negative results are part of the project evidence base, not discarded runs.
